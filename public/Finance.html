The Mathematics of Financial Literacy

1
Coordinate Plotting (Price vs Time)

Every stock ticker, bond yield chart, and option chain sits on a Cartesian grid. The x-axis tracks chronological intervals while the y-axis tracks price or asset valuations. Plotting coordinates makes abstract fluctuations a legible trajectory.

Coordinate: (Time, Valuation)
2
Linear Equations & Asset Beta

The slope of a linear function represents the asset's rate of accumulation. In risk analysis, mapping a stock's excess returns against market benchmarks derives a linear slope representing Beta (β)—a coefficient tracking how violently an asset swings relative to general market fluctuations.

Formula: y = βx + α
3
Systems of Equations (Breakeven)

A system of equations reveals crossovers. In financial modeling, intersecting the cost function and the revenue function solves the system. The intersection coordinate $(x, y)$ shows the breakeven output threshold ($x$) and minimum capital threshold ($y$) where profitability begins.

Crossover: Cost(x) = Revenue(x)
4
Vertices & Efficient Frontiers

In Modern Portfolio Theory, risk and return form a parabolic hyperbola called the Efficient Frontier. The minimum variance portfolio sits exactly at the **vertex** of the curve. Calculating this vertex tells fund managers how to allocate assets to achieve the lowest possible risk.

Vertex of Curve: x = -b / (2a)
